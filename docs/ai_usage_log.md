## 5. The decisions that mattered

Three episodes changed the submitted work in ways worth stating explicitly
rather than leaving inside a table.

**Accepted.** The clean-environment checks in July 2026 surfaced two defects
that would otherwise have reached submission: the ISO 8601 to epoch conversion
assumed the nanosecond datetime resolution of earlier pandas versions and
silently produced values a thousand times too small under pandas 3, and the
question-level response time was being computed as a first-to-last event span
rather than as the documented sum of event durations. I accepted the finding,
wrote the fixes and the tests, and both are covered by the suite.

**Modified.** The review of `src/` in July 2026 identified a coupling between
two detector flags that matched the behaviour of an earlier generator. I
accepted that the coupling was a form of overfitting and removed it, but I
generalised the affected rules myself rather than adopting the specific
replacement suggested, and I re-evaluated on held-out seeds rather than on the
data the coupling had been fitted to.

**Rejected.** The entropy threshold correction motivated by the August
diagnostic raises fabricated per-class recall from 0.70 to 1.00 and drops the
lowest precision anywhere in the round from 0.9570 to 0.8333. I developed it,
froze it, evaluated it once, and decided not to merge it, for the reasons argued
in section 7 of the report: the cost concentrates at low prevalence, which is
the condition that most resembles deployment. It is unmerged on the branch
`fix/entropy-threshold-relative`. Every figure in the report describes the
detector without it.

**Disclosed.** The revision of September 2026 used AI assistance more
extensively than any earlier episode, and the extent is stated here rather
than left to the table. The tool checked every reference against its primary
source, obtained and read the sources that were publicly available, rebuilt
section 2 of the report from them, drafted the comparison in section 7,
appendices E and F and the reflection in section 9.1, wrote the three analysis
scripts and the regression tests added in section 4, and applied the
corrections the supervisor's review listed. Two boundaries were held
throughout. No claim was written about a source that had not been read: where
a source could not be obtained, Appendix E says so and the claim is held at
the level the abstract or publisher record supports. And no number was taken
on trust: every figure in the new tables is reproduced from a file under
`output/`, and the whole of Appendix D was re-run in a clean environment to
confirm it. What remains mine is the obligation to read the drafted prose,
confirm that it says what I am prepared to defend, and obtain the six sources
still listed in Appendix E as unread.

**A note on the entropy branch.** The branch holding the entropy fence
correction, `fix/entropy-threshold-relative`, was created from a rewritten
history and shares no commit with `main`, so it cannot be merged by fast
forward and is preserved as a separate line of development. Its code comment
claimed that the correction reduces to Tukey's fence for a symmetric
distribution; that is wrong, for the reason given in section 7 of the report,
and the comment has been corrected.

## 6. Tests attached to significant outputs

No code-level suggestion entered the submitted work without a test or a recorded
output check behind it. The five defects described in section 4 of the report
are each covered by a test in the suite of forty-three, which passes in full
on Python 3.14.0. The first submission made that claim of a suite of thirty-
seven, and it was wrong: two of the five fixes, the ODK placeholder and the
exclusion of administrative events from section statistics, had no test at
all, and a third covered the preserved integrity flag only indirectly. The
three tests added in September, named in section 4 of the report, make the
statement true. Every quantitative claim in the report resolves
to a file under `output/`, and the commands that regenerate those files are in
Appendix D of the report. The feature coverage table is generated by
`scripts/build_feature_coverage.py` rather than maintained by hand, so it cannot
drift from the code. The component breakdown is recomputed from recorded
predictions rather than from a fresh run, so it describes exactly the runs
reported elsewhere.

## 7. Limitations of this disclosure

Three limitations should be stated. First, entries before 6 July 2026 are
reconstructed from the development history rather than recorded at the time, so
their dates are approximate and their prompt summaries are reconstructions of
substance rather than transcripts. Second, hosted assistant products do not
expose a stable version identifier, so the tool versions for those entries are
not recoverable and this log says so rather than inventing a number. Third, a
log of this kind records episodes of use rather than every keystroke; inline
editor completions in particular are continuous and were not individually
recorded, and section 4 characterises them as a class rather than enumerating
them. Where the record is uncertain I have preferred to say so.
